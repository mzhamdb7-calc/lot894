How to Use This Calculator

Choose a mode

  1. Choose a mode. Current Age calculates age at a reference date; Age on Date uses a target date; Date Difference compares two dates; Next Birthday counts down to the next birthday.
  2. Enter the dates. Age modes use a date of birth plus a reference or target date. Date Difference uses a start and end date.
  3. Review the result. Check the main result and key values.
  4. Open the report. View the detailed breakdown, method and notes.
Tip: Use written dates, such as 6 February 2027, to avoid day/month ambiguity.
How It Works

Calendar calculation

Uses Gregorian dates, actual month lengths and leap-year rules. Exact duration is resolved as completed years, months, then remaining days.

Completed ageFull years reached by the reference or target date.
Exact calendar intervalCompleted years, months and remaining days between two dates.
Elapsed daysEnd date minus start date in calendar days.
Next birthdayFirst birthday anniversary after the reference date, with countdown and turning age.
Completed age = selected year − birth year − 1 if the birthday has not yet occurred in the selected year.
Whole weeks = floor(elapsed days ÷ 7); remaining days = elapsed days mod 7.

For a 29 February birth date, 28 February is used in non-leap years. Official rules may differ.

Assumptions & Limitations

Calculation basis

  • Gregorian calendar. Gregorian month-length and leap-year rules apply throughout the supported range.
  • Date-only input. Time of day, time zone, daylight-saving changes and leap seconds are excluded. Sub-day values are equivalents only.
  • 29 February rule. A 29 February birthday is treated as 28 February in non-leap years. Official rules may differ.
  • Legal age and eligibility. Results are informational and do not determine legal age or eligibility.
  • Inclusive day count. Includes both endpoints, adding one day to the elapsed-day count without changing the exact calendar interval.
For official use, follow the applicable law, contract, policy or institutional rule.
Tips & Common Mistakes

Common checks

  • Check whether the birthday has occurred. Year subtraction alone can overstate completed age by one year.
  • Avoid days ÷ 365 for exact age. It gives an approximation, not exact calendar age.
  • Check date order. The second date must be on or after the first.
  • Check the day-count method. Inclusive counting includes both endpoints.
  • Use written dates. “6 February 2027” avoids day/month ambiguity.
  • Use the correct mode. Age modes require a date of birth; Date Difference can compare any two valid dates.
Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions

Why can calculators differ by one day?

They may use different day-count methods, time zones, timestamps or 29 February rules.

Can I calculate age on a past date?

Yes. The target date must be on or after the date of birth.

Can I calculate a future age?

Yes. Use a future reference or target date.

What if the reference date is a birthday?

Completed age increases that day. Next Birthday uses the following anniversary.

Why not use 30 days per month?

Months vary from 28 to 31 days, so exact duration uses actual month lengths.

Are hours, minutes and seconds exact?

They are 24-hour-day equivalents; birth time is not used.

Can I use this for HR or school eligibility?

Use it as a reference only. The governing law, policy or institution determines eligibility.

Does it account for leap years?

Yes. It uses Gregorian leap-year rules and actual month lengths.

Additional Asian adult health-risk guidance

Some Asian public-health guidance uses BMI 23.0 and 27.5 as additional adult risk action points. This applies to adult interpretation only and does not replace child and teen BMI-for-age percentiles.

FormulaThe BMI equation is the same in both unit systems. The interpretation method changes according to age.
Metric
BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height² (m²)
US units
BMI = 703 × weight (lb) ÷ height² (in²)
Worked example: For 70 kg and 1.75 m, BMI = 70 ÷ 1.75² = 22.9. Adults use the standard BMI ranges; ages 2–19 require BMI-for-age percentile interpretation.
Tips and common mistakesConsistent measurements and correct unit entry matter more than adding extra fields. These checks improve calculation quality without complicating the calculator.

Measurement tips

  • Measure height without shoes and stand upright.
  • Use a recently calibrated scale where possible.
  • Compare results under similar measurement conditions.
  • Retake measurements when a result appears unexpected.
  • Review trends rather than reacting to one isolated result.
  • Interpret BMI together with other health information.

Common mistakes

  • Entering centimetres as metres.
  • Entering decimal feet instead of feet and inches.
  • Mixing pounds with the metric formula.
  • Rounding height before the calculation.
  • Applying adult categories to children or teenagers.
  • Treating BMI as a diagnosis or direct body-fat measurement.
  • Calling the calculated range an “ideal weight.”

Important notice General screening information

BMI is a screening estimate, not a diagnosis.

BMI is calculated from height and weight, but the result must be interpreted for the correct age group. It does not directly measure body fat, muscle mass, fitness, fat distribution, growth quality, or overall health. Use the result as general information and consult a qualified healthcare provider for personal medical advice or concerns.

Calculation Guide

Formula, measurement tips, common mistakes, FAQs, references, and important limitations.

BMI formula

Metric

BMI = kg ÷ m²

US units

BMI = 703 × lb ÷ in²

Worked example: 70 kg ÷ 1.75² = 22.9.

Tips

  • Measure height without shoes.
  • Use a reliable scale.
  • Enter the correct units.
  • Retake unexpected measurements.
  • Compare under similar conditions.
  • Use other health information too.

Common mistakes

  • Entering centimetres as metres
  • Using decimal feet
  • Mixing unit systems
  • Rounding height too early
  • Using adult categories for children
  • Treating BMI as a diagnosis

Quick FAQs

Is BMI a diagnosis?No. It is a screening measure.
Is the formula sex-specific?No, but body composition can differ.
Can children use this result?No. They require BMI-for-age percentiles.
Can muscular adults need context?Yes. BMI does not directly measure body fat.
